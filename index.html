The Aerosol Mask: Unmasking Hidden Greenhouse Warming

SHOW ME Project Exhibit | Econ 238: Environmental Economics

The Core Insight: Fossil fuel combustion releases both greenhouse gases (CO₂), which warm the planet, and sulfate aerosols, which reflect sunlight and cool the planet [IPCC AR6]. Cleaning up air pollution without lowering greenhouse gas concentrations removes this cooling mask, forcing rapid localized warming [Samset et al., 2018].

Greenhouse Forcing (Warming)

+3.84 W/m²

Driven by CO₂, CH₄, and N₂O relative to 1750 baseline [IPCC AR6].

Aerosol Forcing (Cooling Mask)

-1.30 W/m²

Sunlight reflected by atmospheric sulfates and organic carbon [IPCC AR6].

Net Radiative Forcing

+2.54 W/m²

Drives the net +1.30°C warming observed in 2025 [IPCC AR6].

Understanding the Scenarios

  • Baseline (Blue Line): Greenhouse gas concentrations and aerosol cooling remain at current levels. Global mean surface temperature continues its path driven by net radiative forcing (+2.54 W/m²) [IPCC AR6].
  • 50% Aerosol Reduction (Orange Line): Sulfur emissions are halved due to clean air regulations. Eliminating half the aerosol mask (+0.65 W/m² net forcing increase) unleashes ~0.40°C of extra warming by 2035 [Samset et al., 2018].
  • 100% Aerosol Removal (Red Line): All anthropogenic aerosols are phased out. The full greenhouse warming potential (+3.84 W/m²) hits the system, resulting in a rapid surge of ~0.80°C additional warming over baseline [Samset et al., 2018].

Methods & Sources

Radiative forcing parameters are sourced from the IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (AR6 WG1, Chapter 7). Sensitivity runs and climate response timelines are calibrated using transient radiative energy balance models following Samset et al. (2018), "Climate impacts from a removal of anthropogenic aerosols", Geophysical Research Letters.
